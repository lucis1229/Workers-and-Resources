Reading the numbers

Average vs peak

Treatment plants, wells and power stations run continuously, so size them on the average figure. Pipes, pumps, substations and power lines have to survive the evening rush when everyone is home at once — size those on peak.

Water is bursty

A citizen draws 0.045 m³/day only while actually inside their flat. Averaged over a full day of work, school and shopping it lands near 0.01 m³/day — which is why a small water treatment plant covers roughly 12 000 people.

Electricity is the soft number

The game sets power draw per building, not per head, and heating type, industry and night lighting move it a lot. The 0.9 kW average per citizen here is back-calculated from residential blocks; check your substation readout and tune the coefficient to your republic.

Elevation is paid for in pressure

A pipe's pressure drives the flow and buys the climb out of the same purse, so a pipe run near its flow ceiling has nothing left for a hill. Widening the pipe does not help: every size surrenders the same 1.16% of its own limit per metre, which caps any single stage at about 86 m no matter what you lay.

So the sheet answers elevation with parallel runs and pump stages rather than fatter pipe, and it deliberately leaves pressure unspent. Raise the metres and watch the pumps appear — you should never have to check the terrain yourself.

The medium voltage line is the pinch

A substation passes 2.5 MW but the fattest medium voltage line carries 2.20 MW, so a substation worked near its limit needs two feeds. Either run the second line or add another substation and let each sit lower.

Switches are interconnections, despite the name — they cannot cut power. One slot takes the feed and the rest branch onward, so reaching N transformers takes N−1 of the 3-line posts but only a third as many 5-line ones. Both are costed above; pick on price and how much room the pylons need.

Heating counts in two currencies

Buildings ask for hot water in m³ and heating plants are rated in GJ, which is what makes the system hard to read in game. Five m³ of demand per GJ/day of output converts between them, so match exchangers to the m³ figure and plants to the GJ figure.

Bury the pipes. Above ground the heat bleeds away and distant blocks arrive cold; buried runs with a pumping station hold the loss near 15%.

Sewage follows water 1:1

Whatever you pump in comes back out, so a sewage plant is sized off the same demand. Dumping untreated sewage into a river pollutes the water you are drawing downstream and costs you environment penalties — treat before you outfall.
